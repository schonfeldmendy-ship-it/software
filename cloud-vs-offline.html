Should you upload bank statements to a converter website?

Short answer: a converter website processes your statement on its servers, under its privacy policy. An offline converter processes it on your own computer, and nothing is transmitted. For your own statements that is a preference; for client statements it is often the whole decision.

Common questions

Is it safe to upload bank statements to a converter website?

When you upload a statement to a converter website, the file is processed on their servers and handled under their privacy policy — account numbers, balances and transactions included. For your own statements, that is a personal choice. For client statements, many bookkeepers and firms are not comfortable with it, and some engagement agreements do not allow it.

What is the alternative to uploading?

An offline converter: software installed on your own computer that reads the PDF locally. Nothing is transmitted. Offline Bank Statement Converter works this way — it runs with no internet connection at all.

Do offline converters handle scanned paper statements?

This one does — it has built-in OCR for scanned statements, and it flags unreadable rows for review instead of guessing.

How do I know an offline conversion is accurate?

Bank statements print a running balance after every transaction. Offline Bank Statement Converter verifies every extracted row against those printed balances and marks it Verified; anything that does not reconcile is flagged. You can test this on the free tier before paying.
